Make the Wind Mirror

SoarLabs Demo 01, the pitch · written for Lekan · November 12, 2026 · by Claude Fable 5.2 in Claude Code · ten to twelve minutes to read · folded details and the appendix are for reference

My recommendation is one demo, which I am calling the Wind Mirror. A person stands in front of a webcam and sees the wind around her own body. A small learned model says how hard a 100 km/h wind would push her, about a ten-thousandth of a second after it has her pose. When she moves in a way the model has not seen, it says so on screen, hands the question to a real flow solver, and learns the answer. Then Fable 5.2 uses the same check to find a better position than she did.

"Her webcam is a wind tunnel, and the AI tells you when it's guessing."

What was run tonight. A 3D flow solver, checked against textbook shapes, and 485 runs of it. Two small models with a doubt score built from the three signals in your deck. Your performer's three June clips, tracked and solved. An agent run that designed a body position with the check. The doubt score worked on every test but one; that failure is in section 5. Nothing was posted, deployed or sent, and no money was spent.

1. The demo, and the sixty-second cut

Four words I use below. The twin is a simple body made of rounded limbs, posed from her 33 tracked points. The solver is the slow, real computation of air flowing around the twin. The check is the fast learned answer. The doubt score is one number that says how far to trust the check; above a limit the question goes to the solver.

Why this one. It is your product, small and true: the model, the doubt score, the solver behind it, the learning loop and the tool an agent calls all ran tonight. It reads with the sound off, because she moves, the air moves and the number moves in the same frame. It uses the three things you have that the crowded lanes do not: a body tracker, physics, and a person on camera. And in this week's posts nobody shows a design being checked, and nobody shows a model admitting doubt.

2. What it proves about SoarLabs

Each claim is one your deck makes or implies. Every number is from a run on your box tonight; the appendix names the files. "Force error" means the error of the whole force (drag, lift and side force together) as a share of the solver's force.

ClaimEvidenceStatus
A learned model answers far faster than a solver.Solver: 19.8 s per pose on the RTX 3090 Ti. Check: 47 millionths of a second per pose in a batch of 1,000 on one CPU core, with both models and all three doubt signals. That is about 400,000 times faster. A single pose takes 0.1 ms. Posing and measuring the twin first costs about 7 ms; I did not optimise it.measured
Fast models are confidently wrong about what they have not seen.On familiar poses the model's force error is 5.0%. On four kinds of pose left out of training (lying head-first, side-on, back to the wind, upside down) it is 38.9%, worse than a rule of thumb, and nothing in the answer says so.measured
The doubt score knows which answers are wrong.Ranked by doubt score, answers more than 10% wrong sit above the rest 99 times in 100 (100 held-out poses). All three signals contribute: distance from training data 0.99, disagreement between the two models 0.98, the physics check 0.97.measured
The gate passes good answers and stops bad ones.With a 10% tolerance, 36 of 50 familiar poses were answered instantly at 4.0% mean error (worst 11.7%), and none of the 50 never-seen poses. Of 53 answers that were more than 10% wrong, one got through. With the 5% tolerance I wrote first, the gate did not open at all.measured 10%, not 5%
Every solver run makes the model better where it was weak.Her first clip's 77 solver runs, retrained in 8 seconds, cut the error on ordinary frames of her other two clips from 8.2% to 3.6% and opened the gate for all 19. Six solver runs of a never-seen kind of pose cut its error from 56% to 16% (lying head-first).measured
