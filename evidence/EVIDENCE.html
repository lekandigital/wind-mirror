Wind Mirror: the evidence

Every claim the demo makes, with its measurement, the hardware, the baseline and the command that reproduces it. Built 2026-11-12 by python3 evidence/build.py from the result files in evidence/results/. Paths are relative to /Users/lekan/Dev/wind-mirror.

Wind Mirror poses a simple twin of a person in a simulated wind tunnel from a webcam. A small learned model says how hard a 100 km/h wind pushes on the twin and states an error bound. When the bound is over 10% it says it is not sure, sends the body to a real flow solver, and learns the answer. The same engine is offered to an AI agent as tools.

Hardware. Browser and JavaScript figures: Apple M5 MacBook Pro, macOS 26.5, Google Chrome driven headless with the GPU (ANGLE Metal). Solver and training: one NVIDIA RTX 3090 Ti (24 GB) in a Linux box. Nothing was run in a paid cloud.

Error measure used everywhere. "Force error" is the size of the difference between the model's force vector (drag, lift, side force) and the solver's, divided by the size of the solver's force vector. Drag-only error is smaller and is given where it helps.

0.5 msfor the learned model to answer one pose with its bound, in JavaScript on the M5 (0.8 ms measured inside the running page)
5.8 sfor the flow solver it falls back to (3.67 million cells, RTX 3090 Ti to itself; about 13 s when the GPU is shared)
3.4%of 350 "sure" answers were more than 10% wrong, on 500 sealed motion-capture poses. Limit set beforehand: 10%
17%of 150 "not sure" answers were more than 10% wrong on the same poses

The solver's own averaging noise, from the two halves of each run's averaging window, is about 1.0% of the force. One of roughly 3,500 solver runs made today returned non-finite forces (the simulation diverged on one tracked pose); it is excluded and the engine now refuses to learn from such a run. Run again, the same pose diverges again while its neighbours 35 frames either side solve normally, so a particular geometry trips the solver; the cause has not been found.

How to read the loop in the rough cut. The captions are placed by a script, and the take is then checked against its own log. "It is not sure about this one" appears when the page itself sends the question to the solver, which it does only when that pose's own bound is over 10%. "The solver answers" appears when the answer arrives. "Now it is sure" is placed by clip time on the second pass, and the log is checked for any "not sure" reading in that stretch (there is none). The second turn is the same clip replayed, so it shows the model sure about poses it has just learned; learning tested on poses it was not taught is the table below. The label on the page is smoothed over a few frames to stop it flickering, so at the margin it can differ from a single frame's bound.

After every learn call the engine refits the model with the new runs and re-calibrates the bound by leaving each run out in turn; the factor never goes below the one set from held-out pose families. Lying bodies stay hard: in development, 20 solver runs of lying head-first poses took the error on other such poses from 35% to 14% and the model stayed "not sure" throughout.

## 3. What the model got wrong, and whether teaching helped

- **Unsure about every deep tuck at first.** The factory model was sure only of standing and the moderate tuck (run 2); its bounds on runs 3–5 were 13.9–23.7%.
- **One bound broken.** Run 4's actual error was 21.3% against a 17.1% bound, on an answer it had flagged as not sure.
- **Errors were mostly not in drag.** On runs 3–5 drag was within 0.0057 m², yet the reported error was 10.7–21.3%. It appears to include lift: on run 4 the model said 0.0645 m² of lift and the solver found 0.115.
- **Drag misses in the deepest tucks.** Runs 6–9 missed by 0.0104–0.0118 m² in both directions; run 9 was the model's top pick and came out worse than run 6.
- **Teaching helped, locally.** After 10 taught runs the model was sure on two new nearby positions (runs 11–12) and was right to 0.9% and 1.0%. Each round of teaching also moved the search to better positions: best solved went 0.1909 → 0.1571 → 0.1505 → 0.1498 m². That evidence is two points in a neighbourhood dense with taught runs, not a general improvement.

## 3. What the model got wrong, and whether teaching helped

- **"Sure" was not reliable in the tuck region.** Three of the eight sure answers missed their own bound: run0003 (8.5% bound, 14.9% actual), run0005 (9.0%, 11.9%) and run0006 (7.5%, 8.0%).
- **"Unsure" was over-cautious.** All four unsure answers (bounds 11.1–16.8%) were within 2.7–5.0%.
- **The factory model's best sure answer was the wrong family.** It ranked an arms-behind tuck first among sure answers at 0.3137 m²; the solver gave 0.3473 m², worse than the unsure arms-forward crouches.
- **Teaching helped find the region.** Before teaching, none of 4,005 valid arms-forward crouches was sure. After teaching runs 2–4, that family became sure, and the next search led to run0005 at 0.251 m².
- **Teaching tightened later predictions.** After seven taught runs, the five remaining predictions were all inside their bounds (7.0, 3.4, 0.9, 4.4, 2.7%).
- **Teaching did not help rank the last few percent.** The model predicted run0011 better than run0009 (0.2441 m²) and the solver found it worse (0.2541 m²). With all 12 runs taught, it gives the final position as 0.2531 m² ±4.0%, sure, against the solver's 0.2482 m².

6. What already exists

Nothing here is a first, and the page should not be read as claiming one. The closest work found (links and search lists are in NOVELTY_RESEARCH.md in the build folder):

  • A webcam wind tunnel exists in 2D. Blake Sanie's "Aero" (2024) cuts a rider out of a webcam image and runs a 2D lattice-Boltzmann flow in the browser.
  • A learned drag model for posed human bodies exists. Giljarhus and Oggiano (Sports Engineering, 2026): 240 CFD runs of scanned athletes, 2.69% drag-area error, no uncertainty, not live.
  • Confidence scores ship in commercial tools (Ansys SimAI, Altair physicsAI); both say theirs is not an error estimate.
  • Check, solve, learn is a textbook loop in molecular simulation (FLARE, VASP on-the-fly force fields, DP-GEN) and has been shown in CFD (AdaLED). Conformal error bounds for neural PDE models are published (Gopakumar et al.).
  • Agents driving full solvers exist (SimScale's agent, Neural Concept's MCP bridge, AeroAgent).

What was not found: a doubt score tied to an error bound with its false-sure rate published per unseen group; the loop shown live on a moving person; an agent tool that returns that doubt and has a learn call. "Not found" means not in the searches listed there.

7. What is not true, not verified, or weak

  • It does not measure a person. The twin is 19 rounded capsules posed from 33 tracked points. The solver was checked against textbook shapes, never against a wind-tunnel measurement of a human body, and published human drag values could not be traced to primary sources in this build.
  • The stated bound under-covers (section 2): 84–85% against a nominal 90%, and 57–63% on two clips.
  • The air on screen is illustrative. It is a real lattice-Boltzmann simulation, but on about 270,000 cells at a low Reynolds number, slowed down for viewing. The force number never comes from it.
  • The solver itself has error, and it is larger than the model's. The demo's grid is not converged: grids with 3.4 and 8 times as many cells give 4-12% less drag for the same body, and the answer is still falling (section 3). The model's 3-6% error is its distance from this solver, not from the truth. Voxel walls also read a smooth sphere 20% high, and one run in about 3,500 diverges.
  • The tracker is the weakest sensor. Depth from one camera is a guess; when the legs are out of view the twin stands on assumed straight legs and the page says so. Glass-to-glass delay with a real webcam was not measured (recorded clips only, by standing rule).
  • Learning one pose does not teach a family. One solver run makes the model sure only close to that pose.
  • No real phone was tested, only a phone-sized window in Chrome. Real Safari was run once on this Mac (section 1); Firefox was not tried.
  • The live page needs the solver service (the GPU box through an SSH tunnel) for the "ask the solver" step. Without it the page still answers and says when it is not sure, and cannot learn.
